Numerical study

An Integrated CMOS-Compatible Photonic Platform for Room-Temperature Rydberg Electromagnetically Induced Transparency in Rubidium Vapor via Counter-Propagating Stark-Chirped Rapid Adiabatic Passage and Quasiperiodic Control.

A numerical investigation of room-temperature Rydberg electromagnetically induced transparency in thermal rubidium. The study examines transparency-window scaling, residual Doppler effects under different beam geometries, and quasiperiodic control. In the SCRAP-assisted configurations tested, no clear advantage over resonant excitation was demonstrated.

Scope and limitations

The results concern a single-atom theoretical model and numerical simulations. They do not establish a physical implementation or demonstrate a multi-atom gate or photon propagation through a device.

Numerical study

The Ettih Architecture II: Testing Pulse Ordering and Quasiperiodic Dynamical Decoupling — Resolving Two Open Questions from Paper I.

This study examines pulse ordering and quasiperiodic dynamical decoupling. Within the selected time window, the tested counter-intuitive pulse ordering did not improve transfer. The work also compares periodic and Fibonacci-timed control under modeled noise conditions.

Scope and limitations

The conclusions are bounded by the model, noise assumptions and time window examined. They should not be read as experimental evidence or as a claim of device-level performance.

Working principles

Verification
before confidence.

A numerical result is not self-validating. The model, assumptions, units and checks determine what can reasonably be concluded from it.

MethodEvidence · limits · responsibility

01 / Principles

Keep the reasoning
inspectable.

The aim is not to make a result sound certain. It is to make clear what was modeled, what was checked, and where the evidence stops.

01

State the scope

Distinguish the physical system being discussed from the mathematical model used to represent it.

02

Make assumptions visible

Interpret a result in the context of its units, approximations, boundary conditions and parameter choices.

03

Check before concluding

Use appropriate limiting cases, numerical checks and independent references where they are available.

04

Report the boundary

Keep null results and unresolved limitations visible. A simulation is not an experiment, and a model is not a device.

02 / Computational tools

AI as an instrument.
Responsibility stays human.

AI-assisted systems may be used as supporting instruments for technical exploration, explanation and code review. Their outputs are treated as suggestions, not as authoritative results or evidence.

Mathematical reasoning, implementation decisions, corrections and reported findings require careful review. Computational claims must be checked through execution and appropriate validation before publication. Responsibility for the published work and its claims remains mine.
